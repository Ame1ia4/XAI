**Justification of paper topic:** LIME Cost/Quality Scaling on High-Dimensional Tabular Data using multiple different models.

LIME is a popularly used model-agnostic explainability method for black box models. However, the practical cost and reliability of LIME on tabular data is poorly characterised. The runtime of LIME depends on the number of perturbation samples and inference cost of the blackbox model to which its being applied, while the quality of explanations, especially stability and local fidelity, degrade as feature space grows. Studies have examined LIME's instability and proposed variations upon LIME, but there is a lack of guidance on how cost and quality might scale together as dimensionality and model complexity increase. This paper's purpose is to investigate LIME cost/quality scaling across multiple models on large tabular datasets, measuring runtime against stability and fidelity to identify where additional computations stops improving explanation quality.
