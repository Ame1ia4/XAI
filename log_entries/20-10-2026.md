# Dataset Change: Covertype → UCI HAR

## Why we changed dataset
- The paper is about LIME on **high-dimensional** tabular data, but Covertype has only 54 features: 10 numeric and 44 binary soil/wilderness indicators.
- The 17 Oct meeting set the goal of a dataset with features in the hundreds. Calling 54 features "high-dimensional" wouldn't have been credible.

## What we changed to
- **Human Activity Recognition Using Smartphones (UCI HAR)**, UCI dataset 240, DOI 10.24432/C54S4K.
- 10,299 rows, **561 numeric features**, 6 activity classes (walking, walking upstairs, walking downstairs, sitting, standing, laying), no missing values.
- Recorded from 30 volunteers wearing a smartphone on the waist. The features are time- and frequency-domain statistics of the accelerometer and gyroscope signals.
- Source paper: Anguita, Ghio, Oneto, Parra, Reyes-Ortiz, "A Public Domain Dataset for Human Activity Recognition Using Smartphones", ESANN 2013.

## Why HAR
- Hundreds of **real, dense** features. Madelon was rejected because it is synthetic, with 480 of its 500 features being noise.
- Recognised benchmark: one of the 72 datasets in the OpenML-CC18 suite (Bischl et al., NeurIPS 2021).
- Balanced classes (13.7–18.9% each), so 3 explained cases per class works well.
- Small enough (7,352 training rows) that every model trains on the full training set, with no subsampling as there was for Covertype.
- Alternatives rejected:
  - Isolet: 26 classes, so too many explained cases.
  - Bioresponse: 1,776 features, above TabFM's 500 limit.
  - cnae-9: only 1,080 rows.
- All sources were verified. See `lime_study/notes/dataset_justification.md`.

## Why 100, 300 and then 500 features
- The models always train and predict on **all 561 features**. Only the number of features LIME perturbs changes, so model accuracy is the same at every step and only explanation dimensionality varies.
- **Upper bound 500: TabFM's limit.** TabFM's documented `max_num_features` is 500. Going above 500 would mean TabFM sees a feature-subsampled input while the other models don't, which breaks the controlled comparison. 500 still covers 89% of the 561 features.
- **Lower bound 100: the "high-dimensional" floor.** The study is about features in the hundreds, so even the smallest condition meets that requirement. LIME's 10-feature explanation is then only 10% of the perturbed features.
- **Middle 300: even spacing.** Equal steps of 200 give three equally spaced points. Three is the minimum needed to tell a linear trend from a curving one, for example stability that drops sharply and then levels off.
- **Sample-to-feature ratio.** LIME fits a ridge surrogate to `num_samples` perturbations over `d` features. Crossed with the 500–10,000 budgets, the grid runs from **1:1** (500 samples, 500 features, a barely determined surrogate) to **100:1** (10,000 samples, 100 features). LIME's default kernel width also grows with dimension: 0.75·√d gives 7.5, 13.0 and 16.8. Garreau & von Luxburg (AISTATS 2020) show these parameter choices can make LIME miss important features, so these are the regimes where fidelity and stability are expected to change.
- The subsets are **nested** (100 ⊂ 300 ⊂ 500) and chosen with a fixed seed, so each step only adds features and the result is reproducible.
- **Caveat:** no source prescribes exactly 100/300/500. The two ends are justified (TabFM's limit and the "hundreds" floor), but 300 is a design choice for even spacing. Present it in the paper as a design decision.

## Why the split is 70/30 and not 80/20
- It is HAR's **official** split, made by volunteer: 21 volunteers (7,352 rows) for training and 9 other volunteers (2,947 rows) for testing.
- Keeping each person entirely in train or entirely in test prevents leakage. Windows from the same person are very similar, and a random row-level 80/20 split would inflate accuracy.
- Using the published split keeps our accuracies comparable with the original paper (96% with an SVM).
- The 80/20 for Covertype was only our own choice, because Covertype has no official split.
- **The ratio is supported for high-dimensional classifiers.** Dobbin & Simon (2011, *BMC Medical Genomics* 4:31, DOI 10.1186/1755-8794-4-31) found that giving about 2/3 of cases to training "was close to optimal for reasonable sized datasets (n ≥ 100) with strong signals (85% or greater accuracy)". HAR meets both conditions (n = 10,299; our models reach 93–95%), and 70/30 is close to 2/3.
- **Splitting by subject is supported.** Saeb et al. (2017, *GigaScience* 6(5):1–9, DOI 10.1093/gigascience/gix019) show that splitting by record instead of by subject "often massively overestimates the prediction accuracy". Gholamiangonabadi et al. (2020, *IEEE Access* 8:133982–133994) argue the same for HAR specifically.

## What else changed
- Covertype has been removed from `lime_study/` completely. Its old results are still in git history (commit `58c9cb4`).
- 6 classes × 3 cases gives 18 explained cases. That is **1,350 explanations per model** (previously 1,575) and 4,050 across all three models.
- No categorical features any more: all 561 features are continuous and quartile-discretised by LIME.
- Logistic Regression now standardises all features (previously only the 10 numeric ones). It converges in 119 iterations.
- The data is downloaded automatically from UCI on the first run, and the file's hash is recorded with the results.
- First quick-run test accuracies: Logistic Regression 0.955, XGBoost 0.941, Random Forest 0.928.
