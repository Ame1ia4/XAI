# AI Usage — LIME Cost and Quality Scaling Project

ChatGPT was used as a **support tool** throughout this Explainable AI (XAI) project investigating how LIME's computational cost and explanation quality change with sample count, feature dimensionality, and model choice. This summary covers only project-related interactions.

| Area | Typical prompts / requests | AI assistance provided |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Literature review** | Asked AI to find and explain relevant LIME/XAI papers, summarise key findings in plain English, and assess their relevance to our study. | Provided paper summaries, explanations of concepts and limitations, and guidance on how previous work relates to cost, fidelity, and stability. |
| **Dataset selection and experiment planning** | Asked AI to recommend large, accessible tabular datasets and help judge whether they were suitable for testing sample-size and feature-scaling effects. | Suggested candidate datasets, discussed practical advantages and limitations, and helped clarify experimental variables and comparisons. |
| **Coding and troubleshooting** | Shared Python errors and asked how to run the study or resolve missing packages and environment issues. | Explained likely causes and suggested commands or steps to investigate and fix the issues. |
| **Results and critical review** | Asked AI to explain graphs, check interpretations, question whether changes were consistent with the research questions, and identify possible issues. | Offered interpretations of runtime, stability, and fidelity results; highlighted caveats, inconsistencies, and checks worth making. |
| **Paper writing and editing** | Asked AI to review or shorten sections, improve clarity, refine conclusions, and keep claims aligned with the results and page limit. | Suggested clearer wording, condensed drafts, and ways to present the findings without overstating them. |
| **Presentation preparation** | Asked AI for slide summaries, graph explanations, speaking points, and likely questions. | Helped turn technical findings into concise slides and presentation notes. |

## Prompting approach

Prompts were mostly **exploratory** ("suggest datasets or papers"), **explanatory** ("explain this result or error"), **evaluative** ("does this interpretation hold up?"), and **iterative editing** ("shorten or improve this section"). Follow-up prompts were used to challenge suggestions, request corrections, and adjust the level of detail.

## Human oversight

AI responses were treated as **suggestions and explanations, not experimental evidence**. The study's measured results came from the project experiments; interpretations, technical changes, and final written claims required review against those results and the original sources.
