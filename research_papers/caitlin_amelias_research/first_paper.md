**Title:** Towards Robust and Accurate Stability Estimation of Local Surrogate Models in Text-based Explainable AI


**Link:** https://arxiv.org/html/2501.02042

**Key Takeaways:** This paper discusses the stability and robustness of XAI methods under adversarial attacks (deliberate manipulations that trick or distort the interpretability tools), and proposes a weighting scheme for text-based data that provides improved estimates of actual weakness of XAI methods to adversarial examples. Ultimately, LIME is establised as inherently unstable in its sampling process for text data.

**Abstract:** Recent work has investigated the concept of adversarial attacks on explainable AI (XAI) in the NLP domain with a focus on examining the vulnerability of local surrogate methods such as Lime to adversarial perturbations or small changes on the input of a machine learning (ML) model. In such attacks, the generated explanation is manipulated while the meaning and structure of the original input remain similar under the ML model. Such attacks are especially alarming when XAI is used as a basis for decision making (e.g., prescribing drugs based on AI medical predictors) or for legal action (e.g., legal dispute involving AI software). Although weaknesses across many XAI methods have been shown to exist, the reasons behind why remain little explored. Central to this XAI manipulation is the similarity measure used to calculate how one explanation differs from another. A poor choice of similarity measure can lead to erroneous conclusions about the stability or adversarial robustness of an XAI method. Therefore, this work investigates a variety of similarity measures designed for text-based ranked lists referenced in related work to determine their comparative suitability for use. We find that many measures are overly sensitive, resulting in erroneous estimates of stability. We then propose a weighting scheme for text-based data that incorporates the synonymity between the features within an explanation, providing more accurate estimates of the actual weakness of XAI methods to adversarial examples. Source codes will be made publicly available.


**AI Summary:** "Towards Robust and Accurate Stability Estimation of Local Surrogate Models in Text-based Explainable AI" (arXiv 2501.02042)

*Summary:* The paper studies adversarial attacks on LIME explanations for text classifiers. Its main point is that the similarity measure used to compare two explanations (Jaccard, Kendall's tau, Spearman's footrule, rank-biased overlap) can strongly skew conclusions about stability. Kendall's tau reports near-100% attack success at every threshold. Others are too coarse. The authors propose synonymity weighting, which compares perturbed words by how semantically close they are, rather than treating them as strict mismatches. This sharply cuts the exaggerated instability for Jaccard and Spearman. Experiments use two short-text datasets with DistilBERT.

*Relevance:* low to moderate, and mostly indirect.

- What does not transfer. It is text-only. The synonymity weighting depends on word embeddings, so it has no direct tabular equivalent. The authors also say their conclusions about which measures work may not carry over to other XAI methods.
- What does transfer: choosing a stability metric. If you measure quality as the stability of LIME's ranked feature lists across repeated runs, which is a natural choice for tabular data, this paper warns that the metric you choose can change your conclusions. Kendall and unweighted Jaccard are likely to overstate instability. RBO is described as a good balance between sensitivity and result quality, though it needs its weighting parameter tuned. This is a point for your methodology section.
- Cost evidence. The authors report that the IMDB experiments were computationally infeasible, at roughly 125 days of continuous GPU computation, because generating adversarial examples requires repeated LIME runs. This is a data point on how explanation cost compounds, but it is for text, not tabular data.
- A side observation. They note that LIME's own sampling instability alone can make some similarity measures register "attacks" even with little perturbation. That is a reminder to separate sampling noise from true instability when you vary the number of samples.

**Paper Value:** This paper is not of much value given our topic as it simply establishes LIME's instability then primarily discusses a proposed weighting scheme to help identify weaknesses of XAI methods against adversarial attacks.
